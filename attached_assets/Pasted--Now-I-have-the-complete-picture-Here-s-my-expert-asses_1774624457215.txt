The core problem is clear: your site is a Single Page Application (SPA) that serves an empty <div id="root"></div> to regular crawlers.

When Google, Bing, or any search engine visits your pages, here's what they see for every single URL:

  <div id="root"></div>

That's it. No content. No headings. No text. Nothing to index.

You do have a bot-detection layer (the SEO renderer) that serves pre-rendered HTML to crawlers it recognizes by user agent. When Googlebot identifies itself, it gets proper content. But here's why that's still failing you:

1. Google doesn't always identify as Googlebot. Google uses rendering bots, headless Chrome, and other crawlers that may not match your bot pattern. When they don't match, they get the empty shell. Google has also said publicly that cloaking (serving different content to bots vs. users) can be flagged, even when well-intentioned.

2. Your individual news articles aren't in the sitemap. Your sitemap has 187 URLs, but zero individual news article pages (/news/[slug]). You have 28 articles that Google doesn't even know exist. Same problem if newer guides aren't being added dynamically.

3. Every page shares the same meta tags. When a regular browser (not a recognized bot) hits /news, /guides/password-managers, or /shield, they all return the identical homepage title, description, and OG tags. Google sees duplicate metadata across your entire site.

4. No structured data for regular visitors. The JSON-LD schema markup (breadcrumbs, FAQ, article schema) only exists in the bot-rendered version. Google's rendering service won't always see it.

5. Some guide URLs are 404-ing for bots. I tested guide URLs from your sitemap and some returned proper content, but variations in slug format return 404. If Google followed a link with a slightly different slug, it gets a dead page.

Here's what needs to happen, in priority order:

Priority 1 — Server-Side Rendering (SSR) or Static Pre-rendering. This is the real fix. Every page needs to return actual HTML content to every visitor, not just recognized bots. Options:

Migrate to Next.js (big lift but gold standard)
Add a pre-rendering service like Rendertron or Prerender.io that caches rendered pages and serves them universally
At minimum, inject page-specific meta tags into the HTML template on the server side for every request, not just bot requests
Priority 2 — Dynamic sitemap with all content. Your sitemap needs to automatically include every news article, every guide, every scam intelligence page. Right now you're hiding your best indexable content from search engines.

Priority 3 — Stop serving different content to bots vs. users. Google's guidelines are clear: the same content should be served to everyone. Your current approach is technically "dynamic rendering" which Google tolerates but doesn't love. The more your bot-rendered HTML diverges from what the JavaScript renders, the more risk you carry.

Priority 4 — Unique meta tags per page at the HTML level. Even without full SSR, the server can read the URL path and inject the correct title, description, and canonical URL into the HTML before sending it. This is a much smaller change than full SSR.

Priority 5 — Submit and monitor in Google Search Console. If you haven't already, verify the site, submit the sitemap, and use the URL inspection tool to see exactly what Google is rendering for your pages. That will confirm whether the bot detection is actually working in practice.

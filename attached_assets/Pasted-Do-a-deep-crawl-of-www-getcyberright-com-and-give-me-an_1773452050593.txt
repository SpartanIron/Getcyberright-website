Replit-hosted React apps are pure client-side rendered SPAs by default. When Googlebot, social media crawlers, or my fetcher hit your URL, they get an empty <div id="root"></div> and a bundle of JavaScript. Most crawlers do not execute JavaScript. So your site is a blank page to the entire internet except humans with modern browsers who wait for the JS to load.

This is not a minor issue. It is the foundational reason your site is invisible.

The Core Tradeoff You Face

You have three paths, and they escalate in effort:

Path 1: Pre-rendering service (fastest fix, lowest effort). Add a pre-rendering layer like Prerender.io or react-snap that generates static HTML snapshots of your pages and serves them to bots. This gets you indexed within days without rewriting anything. Downside: it is a band-aid, not architecture.

Path 2: Migrate to Next.js on Replit (moderate effort, correct long-term answer). Next.js gives you SSR and static site generation out of the box. Replit supports Next.js deployments. You would rebuild your components into the Next.js page/app router structure. Your existing React components port over with minimal changes since Next.js is just React with a rendering layer on top. This is what I would recommend given your budget constraints and the fact that you are already comfortable in Replit.

Path 3: Move off Replit entirely (highest effort, most control). Deploy on Vercel, Railway, or Cloudflare Pages with Next.js or Astro. Better performance, better CDN, better control over headers, caching, and edge rendering. This is where you should end up eventually, but it is not the urgent fix.

Then the problem is not the framework. The problem is your configuration. Next.js gives you SSR for free, which means something is actively breaking it. Let me narrow this down.

Most likely culprits, in order of probability:

1. Everything is wrapped in "use client" directives. If your page components (especially page.tsx or page.jsx in the app router, or your pages in the pages directory) are marked "use client", Next.js skips server-side rendering for those components entirely. The HTML sent to crawlers is effectively empty. This is the single most common mistake developers make with Next.js App Router. Check your top-level page files right now. If they start with "use client", that is your problem.

2. Content is loaded entirely via useEffect or client-side fetch calls. If your hero text, section content, or any visible content is fetched inside useEffect hooks or client-side API calls rather than being hardcoded or fetched server-side (via getServerSideProps, getStaticProps, or async server components), crawlers see nothing. The HTML ships empty and waits for JavaScript to populate it.

3. Replit's deployment is not running the Next.js server correctly. Replit sometimes serves the app in development mode or misconfigures the production build. If the deployment is running next dev instead of next build && next start, SSR behavior can be inconsistent and performance tanks. Check your Replit run command.

4. Missing or broken next.config.js output settings. If you have output: 'export' in your Next.js config, you have turned the entire app into a static export with no SSR at all. This would produce the exact symptoms I observed: a title tag in the HTML head but zero rendered body content.
